# Narrative Foundation

Status: **Working Canon Checkpoint — Review Before Story Implementation**

This document defines the campaign-wide narrative direction for **The Forsaken
Tenet**. It establishes the story's identity, themes, tone, canon boundaries,
reveal schedule, and authoring rules before the opening and middle game receive
a larger content pass.

The game began as a systems-first homage to **Mordor: The Depths of Dejenol**:
enter town, prepare, descend, survive, return, and grow strong enough to go
deeper. Class promotions, special combat skills, class identities, authored
quests, and the Vesperion/Voluntas endgame have since moved it toward a more
complete RPG. The story should strengthen that evolution without discarding the
dungeon-crawl rhythm that made the project worth building.

This document owns creative campaign direction. Existing runtime and
compatibility contracts remain owned by:

- STORY_AND_ENDGAME_DESIGN.md for the implemented Vesperion/Voluntas route;
- QUEST_STORY_INTEGRATION_DESIGN.md for quest staging and persisted quest state;
- PRESENTATION_ASSET_DESIGN_GATES.md for story-card and presentation behavior;
- DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md for implementation priority.

Where current content conflicts with this direction, the conflict becomes
narrative debt. This document does not silently change shipped mechanics, map
routes, rewards, save fields, or boss gates.

## Narrative Identity

**The Forsaken Tenet** is a dark-fantasy dungeon-crawl RPG about a frontier town
living beside an impossible descent and a hero whose repeated choices restore a
principle erased from history.

Its story should be experienced through a repeating rhythm:

1. Silvana presents a human need, disagreement, loss, or unanswered report.
2. The dungeon provides evidence that complicates the town's understanding.
3. Exploration and combat force the hero to act without complete certainty.
4. A boss, relic, or special location embodies a distorted principle.
5. The hero returns to a town that remembers what happened.
6. Recurring characters interpret the same event differently.
7. The next descent begins with more knowledge and higher personal stakes.

The player should still be free to explore, pursue side quests, experiment with
classes, and spend long stretches in the dungeon. The story should create
meaning around that play rather than repeatedly interrupting it.

## Central Dramatic Question

The campaign asks:

> Is free will worth the suffering it makes possible?

Vesperion's answer is no. He believes choice inevitably produces grief,
betrayal, fear, inequality, and regret. His mercy is the removal of uncertainty:
a perfectly ordered world in which no one can choose badly because no one can
meaningfully choose at all.

The story's answer is not that freedom prevents suffering. Joffrey still dies.
The Waitress still grieves. The Acolyte still fails. Silvana does not become
whole merely because Vesperion is defeated.

The answer is that without choice there can be no courage, love, trust,
repentance, identity, sacrifice, growth, or meaning. Voluntas is worth
preserving not because every choice is good, but because goodness without the
ability to refuse it is only obedience.

No legitimate player class, promotion, Guardian answer, or role-played motive
is the one morally correct expression of Voluntas. The campaign affirms chosen
identity rather than prescribing one approved identity.

## Narrative Pillars

### The Descent Must Retain Its Mystery

The dungeon is not simply a sequence of monster habitats. Its rooms preserve
damaged evidence: patrol marks, incompatible maps, abandoned equipment,
Guardian symbols, Acolyte fragments, altered memories, and traces of people who
made consequential decisions there.

Answers should arrive through accumulation. Early witnesses disagree. Later
discoveries reveal that several contradictory accounts were partial views of
the same truth.

### Silvana Is The Emotional Anchor

The dungeon creates danger; Silvana makes that danger matter.

The town should evolve after major bosses, relic discoveries, deaths, and route
revelations. A changing line at the tavern, a new name in the barracks ledger,
an empty chair, a repaired wall, or an NPC who no longer tells the same joke can
carry more weight than a long exposition scene.

Returning to town should provide emotional progression as well as healing,
shopping, quests, and storage.

### The Six Principles Must Shape The Whole Journey

The relics are not merely keys carried until the final room. Each one anchors a
story arc in both the dungeon and Silvana:

- **Triangulus — Self:** identity is chosen, not merely assigned by name, body,
  memory, race, class, or circumstance.
- **Quadrata — Order:** structure can protect people, but order without consent
  becomes a cage.
- **Hexagonum — Nature:** instinct begins life, but hunger, fear, strength, and
  weakness do not determine what a life must mean.
- **Luna — Love:** love requires freedom; possession, compulsory sacrifice, and
  obligation can imitate it without becoming it.
- **Polaris — Guidance:** a guide must leave room for doubt, refusal, and a path
  the traveler can call their own.
- **Infinitas — Endurance:** continuing matters only while stopping, resting, or
  choosing another road remains possible.

Their meanings should be foreshadowed before The Liminal Gap. The Liminal
trials then clarify a pattern the player has already lived instead of
introducing six philosophies at the end.

### Build Identity Is Narrative Identity

Class selection, promotions, Class Ring awakening, learned skills, and chosen
combat style are expressions of the hero's decisions. The story should
acknowledge them without treating one build as canon.

The player character should not receive an overly fixed biography. Their
reason for answering Silvana's plea may be expressed through a small number of
remembered role-playing choices, but routine connective writing must remain
compatible with any race, sex, base class, promotion path, and legal build.

### Consequences Should Be Visible Without Requiring A Branching Epic

The critical route may remain structurally stable while choices alter:

- how the hero explains a decision;
- which NPC interpretation receives support;
- optional dialogue and later echoes;
- what the Reflection says;
- how townspeople remember a scene;
- whether secondary help, testimony, or context is available;
- presentation of a fixed tragic outcome.

A choice is not meaningful merely because it changes a reward. A choice can be
meaningful when the world remembers it and responds honestly.

### The Antagonist Must Be Present Before He Is Named

Vesperion should not become relevant only when the final door opens.

As the Busboy, he occupies the role people overlook. He listens, remembers,
offers apparently sympathetic observations, and asks questions that gradually
expose his belief that people would be happier if painful choices were removed.
His lines must work as ordinary dialogue on a first playthrough and as
deliberate ideological testing in retrospect.

He should not secretly cause every hardship. The dungeon, monsters, human fear,
ambition, grief, and error are real. Vesperion observes, pressures, withholds,
and exploits. Making him the sole cause of all suffering would accidentally
support his claim that removing one will can solve the world.

## Tone Contract

The primary tone is **grounded dark fantasy with restrained gallows humor**.

Silvana is frightened and weary, but it is not humorless. People living near a
lethal dungeon make jokes, use ridiculous nicknames, drink too much, tell bad
stories, and give dangerous errands memorable titles. That texture should
remain.

### Humor Belongs In

- optional side-quest titles;
- tavern banter and background patrons;
- the Drunkard's voice;
- item names and eccentric merchant behavior;
- occasional monster descriptions;
- incidental references that make sense inside the setting;
- relief after tension rather than during a revelation.

### Humor Must Recede During

- deaths, funerals, and grief;
- Guardian or relic revelations;
- the Waitress/Joffrey tragedy;
- Acolyte testimony;
- the false final and Liminal transition;
- Reflection scenes;
- Vesperion's argument and the ending.

Pop-culture references may remain, but the game should naturalize them. A
reference should still read as a plausible phrase, title, rumor, or joke in
Silvana even when the player recognizes its source. The narrator and characters
should not wink at an external audience or undermine the reality of the world.

Quest titles may be playful while their start, help, and completion text treats
the underlying people and danger sincerely.

## Canon Baseline

The following points are settled unless a later story decision explicitly
revises them.

### Silvana And The Layered Dungeon

- Silvana is a rain-dark frontier town at the kingdom's edge, built directly
  above a layered underground complex rather than beside an unrelated ruin.
- The upper works served practical purposes within living memory: storage,
  drainage or water management, mining, refuge, burial, defense, and controlled
  access beneath the settlement. The exact division among those functions can
  follow the existing town map and opening-story needs.
- Those practical works reused a much older buried stronghold or temple-city.
  Its builders knew that still older sacred structures continued below, but
  their knowledge degraded into ritual, partial records, and incompatible
  legends.
- At the deepest historical foundation lies the Guardian complex built around
  the seven principles. Later cultures occupied, expanded, fortified, mined,
  sealed, and misinterpreted what they inherited.
- The Hollowing damaged the boundaries between realms and opened inter-realm
  breaches through the descent.
- The initial contamination was an unplanned consequence of that fracturing,
  not a coordinated invasion, migration, or diplomatic contact. Early beings,
  materials, and spaces crossed because the boundaries failed.
- Once factions discovered usable routes, they began exploiting them for
  harmful or desperate purposes. Those later crossings can be intentional
  without making the original contamination planned.
- The first deliberate exploitation the hero encounters is a mixed raider
  compact establishing a foothold in the dungeon. Mercenaries, outcasts, and
  supernatural predators recruited from several groups unite around plunder,
  captives, and opportunity rather than a shared ancestry or moral nature.
- The compact's immediate objective is extractive. It uses the foothold to strip
  Guardian structures and older dungeon layers of relics, unusual materials,
  and saleable knowledge, then move the spoils through the stable anchor.
- The compact entered without prior knowledge of the Guardian legacy, targeted
  maps, or an informed patron. It began systematic plunder only after
  accidentally discovering that the ruins contained valuable remains.
- The first visible consequence is an overdue expedition from Silvana. The hero
  follows its route through looted chambers, abandoned equipment, and signs of
  struggle to surviving captives and the compact's foothold.
- The compact keeps those explorers alive for their expertise. It forces them
  to guide raiders through local routes, bypass hazards, identify promising
  artifacts, and help dismantle unfamiliar structures safely.
- Breaking the foothold requires an irreversible choice between lives now and
  future safety. The compact's dismantling has damaged Guardian containment
  structures that had limited the local effects of the Hollowing.
- That damage is beginning a breach cascade: smaller inter-realm tears multiply,
  move through nearby levels, and release creatures or hazardous environments.
  The hero can rescue the captives or stabilize the containment structures, but
  cannot complete both in time.
- Rescuing the captives allows the cascade to spread and create visible later
  harm. Containing it costs the captives' lives. Silvana remembers both what the
  hero protected and what the choice left unprotected.
- No branch may destroy a required relic, erase an essential campaign truth, or
  block the critical path. The sacrifice changes survivors, later hazards, and
  testimony rather than determining whether the story remains completable.
  Sympathetic underworld factions appear later and demonstrate that the compact
  does not represent the realm as a whole.
- The resulting breach network is hybrid. Exactly two major breaches remain
  anchored and stable enough for repeat two-way travel between the dungeon and
  entire side realms, supporting recurring routes, factions, and quests.
- One anchored route leads to the Realm of Cambion. The other leads to a
  chthonic underworld containing both physical subterranean civilizations and
  supernatural domains; its proper name remains to be settled.
- Its embodied peoples may include dark elves, dwarves, and other underground
  cultures, while demons, fiends, monsters, and other supernatural beings also
  inhabit or claim parts of the realm. Neither category is assumed to be a
  single culture, allegiance, or moral alignment.
- Power is fragmented among settlements, clans, courts, and lairs rather than
  held by one realm-wide sovereign or by either broad category. These groups
  compete, trade, negotiate, and sometimes cooperate; alliances and rivalries
  can cross the physical-supernatural divide.
- This underworld is not the universal destination of every dead soul. Access
  to it does not make resurrection routine, permit the recovery of any chosen
  dead person, or weaken the permanence of Joffrey's death.
- The chthonic realm is distinct from The Liminal Gap. The former is a living
  external realm reached through an anchored breach; the latter remains the
  exceptional threshold used by the endgame's death-and-return sequence.
- Smaller tears open unpredictably, shift location, or collapse. Damage to
  surviving Guardian containment can cause a local cascade in which those tears
  multiply and migrate through adjacent levels. Creatures, travelers, rooms,
  and materials passing through them may be stranded or severed from their
  realms of origin.
- Many unrelated forces later crossed the breaches independently: beasts,
  refugees, raiders, cults, explorers, and other opportunists from several
  realms. Some crossings were deliberate, while others were accidental.
- No single realm, invasion, or commanding power controls the contamination,
  and Vesperion did not direct everything that passed through the breaches.
- Those crossings folded creatures, materials, rooms, and possibly entire side
  spaces from shadow realms, underworlds, and other planes into structures that
  were never designed to contain them.
- The corruption explains why architecture, ecosystems, technology, and
  metaphysical rules can change abruptly without requiring one civilization to
  have built every floor.
- Silvana knew and used only the uppermost layers. The full depth and sacred
  purpose had been lost before Vesperion deliberately opened the current deeper
  threshold.
- He engineered the opening to attract explorers, draw them into recovering
  the six relics, and provoke a final confrontation over Voluntas.
- This plan does not mean Vesperion created the dungeon, caused every danger
  within it, or controlled the independent forces exploiting its inter-realm
  breaches.
- Treasure seekers, soldiers, scholars, pilgrims, opportunists, and desperate
  people followed the new breach.
- Most who descend do not return unchanged; many do not return at all.
- The chthonic underworld, its physical peoples, and its supernatural powers
  still need proper names and specific factions and alliances. The raider
  compact's name and leadership, the long-term consequences of either
  sacrifice, and each later faction's harmful or desperate reason for crossing
  also remain to be settled. Which other recurring groups deserve names, and what
  conditions stabilize or collapse smaller tears, also remain active canon
  decisions. Early characters do not possess authoritative
  answers or know Vesperion's role in the opening.

### The Six Guardians And Voluntas

- The ancient civilization discovered the seven principles while rebuilding
  from a devastating internal conflict and social near-collapse.
- Survivors studied the failures that produced the crisis and formalized the
  principles as mutually balancing safeguards. No one principle was meant to
  rule without the restraint and perspective of the other six.
- The people recognized as the original Guardians had already embodied the
  principles through consequential choices during the collapse. Their lives
  provided the evidence from which the principles were named.
- Reconstruction leaders formally recognized those seven crisis exemplars and
  created the Guardian offices around them. They did not select the originals
  through an election, trial, or supernatural sign.
- The Guardians were spiritual custodians rather than civil rulers. They
  protected the relics, preserved the historical record, and interpreted and
  transmitted the balanced teachings.
- They could advise, warn, and challenge the civilization's conscience, but
  they held no formal power to govern, veto policy, command officials, or
  enforce their interpretations.
- After the original exemplars, each Guardian alone selected and trained a
  successor. No council, community vote, or supernatural sign confirmed the
  transfer; every succession depended on personal judgment and intimate trust.
- The core teachings survived, but generations of private instruction subtly
  narrowed each lineage's emphasis. No single transfer constituted a betrayal;
  the cumulative drift made the seven traditions easier to treat as separate
  doctrines rather than a balanced whole.
- Later political movements exaggerated those differences into incompatible
  orthodoxies. The final six custodians still rejected the resulting factions
  and the civil war.
- Verified history does not treat the principles or the Guardians as a divine
  creation or gift.
- Later religions, including the Church of Elysia, recast that history as the
  bestowal of sacred principles by a deity. This theology neither proves nor
  disproves Elysia's existence; it is not a literal account of the tenets'
  historical origin.
- The known relics preserve six principles: Self, Order, Nature, Love,
  Guidance, and Endurance.
- The six are safeguards and witnesses, not sufficient answers by themselves.
- Every principle becomes hollow when separated from meaningful choice.
- Voluntas is the forgotten seventh principle: Choice and Free Will.
- Voluntas is not a seventh collectible relic, throne, weapon, or external
  power. It exists wherever a soul can freely decide.
- The original Guardian of Voluntas personally prepared Vesperion as the
  office's first successor.
- Vesperion legitimately inherited the custodial office. His later rejection
  of choice betrayed the founder who trusted him as well as the principle
  itself.
- The Guardian office conferred no inherent immortality. Vesperion remained
  mortal until the Hollowing.
- Through the Hollowing, he removed the principle from recorded history and
  left the remaining six easier to convert into instruments of control.
- That erasure allowed later traditions to remember only six divine gifts,
  concealing the missing Guardian inside sincere inherited belief.

### Vesperion

- Vesperion is associated with the Evening Star, radiant twilight, silence,
  certainty, control, and mercy through removal of choice.
- He believes his position is compassionate.
- The reconstructed civilization later divided into rival movements that each
  treated one tenet as a complete answer rather than part of a balanced whole.
- Those movements converted valid principles into competing orthodoxies,
  weaponized them against one another, and drove the civilization into a second
  civil war and social collapse.
- The other six Guardian traditions rejected the absolutist factions but
  withdrew into the Guardian complex. They sealed the relics, records, and
  teachings, prioritizing preservation over direct intervention while the
  civilization collapsed outside.
- The withdrawal did not endorse any faction or grant the custodians civil
  authority. It preserved the balanced tradition at the cost of leaving the
  war's outcome to people who had already rejected that balance.
- When Vesperion began the Hollowing, the six withdrawn custodians chose to hold
  the seals and died preventing the breach from immediately consuming the
  Guardian complex and its relics.
- Vesperion understood beforehand that they would hold the seals and that doing
  so would kill them. Their deaths were not the Hollowing's primary purpose, but
  he knowingly accepted them as a necessary cost and proceeded.
- Before death, each custodian voluntarily impressed a memory-echo into their
  relic. Those echoes later form the six Liminal trials; they are neither
  imprisoned souls nor living ancient Guardians.
- Vesperion interpreted both the recurring conflict and the withdrawal as proof
  that voluntary restraint could never work. He mistook the civilization's
  decision to sever the principles from balance and choice for proof that
  meaningful choice itself was the defect.
- Vesperion was mortal until the Hollowing tore him from natural life, made him
  ageless, and bound his continued existence to the inter-realm breaches he
  created.
- The Hollowing fractured the boundaries between realms, but Vesperion did not
  command every force that later crossed them.
- He deliberately opened the deeper threshold beneath Silvana to attract
  explorers, recover the six relics through their efforts, and provoke a final
  confrontation over Voluntas.
- He is not a cackling destroyer and should not speak like the legacy Devil.
- He uses the Busboy disguise to remain near the hero and observe how grief,
  blame, love, ambition, and uncertainty shape decisions.
- He pressures and exploits the Waitress/Joffrey tragedy but does not make
  their love or pain unreal.
- His final defeat preserves Voluntas; it does not eliminate ordinary grief or
  human wrongdoing.

### The Hooded Figure

- The Hooded Figure is not Vesperion.
- The figure is a surviving Witness of Voluntas and a deliberately ominous
  misdirect.
- Their ethical distinction from Vesperion is behavioral: they may warn,
  withhold, test, and invite, but they must ultimately leave decisions to the
  hero.
- Their wounded Liminal identity, Witness reveal, and later angelic
  confirmation remain the current route.
- A personal angelic name is not yet canon.

### The Acolyte

- The Acolyte is a former hero who followed the relic path and failed.
- They came to believe Vesperion's promise that surrendering choice would end
  suffering.
- Their surrender felt peaceful before it became empty.
- They are a tragic ideological mirror rather than another required boss.
- Their existence should be foreshadowed through fragments, traces, altered
  records, or remembered actions across the main descent.
- The exact era, identity, class history, relationship to the Hooded Figure,
  and degree of direct contact with Vesperion remain development questions.

### Joffrey And The Waitress

- Their love and grief are real.
- Joffrey must exist as a person before he becomes a body, letter, or symbol.
- His fatal decision must remain meaningfully his even if Vesperion pressures,
  deceives, or exploits him.
- The Waitress must have desires, judgment, anger, and relationships beyond
  receiving news of Joffrey.
- The tragedy is not reversed by Voluntas or the ending.
- The hero may affect how truth is discovered, delivered, understood, or
  remembered without requiring the critical path to make Joffrey's survival
  variable in the first narrative pass.

## Cast Obligations

Current stable internal identifiers may remain while a later cast pass decides
display names. Story text should nevertheless treat each central role as a
specific person.

- **The Hero:** player-authored identity; proves Voluntas through accumulated
  choices rather than a fixed prophetic destiny.
- **The Sergeant:** maintains the ledgers, balances public safety against the
  need to keep sending people below, and must reckon with the danger of turning
  protection into command.
- **The Barkeep:** keeper of ordinary memory and the social center of Silvana;
  notices who returns, who is missing, and what the town refuses to say aloud.
- **The Waitress:** emotional center of the Joffrey arc; capable of hope,
  frustration, work, love, blame, grief, and eventual survival.
- **Joffrey:** an active participant in Silvana's defense whose motives and
  choices must be witnessed before his death.
- **The Busboy/Vesperion:** quiet observer and ideological interrogator whose
  apparent empathy becomes disturbing in retrospect.
- **The Hooded Figure:** feared guide whose respect for refusal gradually
  distinguishes them from the apparent kindness of the Busboy.
- **The Drunkard:** humor, memory, and survivor guilt; his later attempt at
  sobriety can embody endurance as an active choice rather than a joke ending.
- **Mara Vale and Seraphine Voss:** useful bridges between ordinary side quests,
  exploration knowledge, and the dungeon's changing interpretation.
- **The Acolyte:** absent predecessor whose trail creates a second, failed
  version of the player's journey.

Merchants, priests, soldiers, scientists, Nimue, Merzhin, the Jester, the Red
Dragon, and other existing figures remain available supporting cast. Their
content should be assigned to the critical route, a supporting arc, or optional
texture rather than competing indiscriminately for main-story status.

## Narrative Delivery Hierarchy

### Critical Path

The critical path owns the Six Relics, the changing understanding of the
Guardians, the false final, The Liminal Gap, Voluntas, Reflection, Vesperion,
and the ending.

### Supporting Character Arcs

Joffrey and the Waitress, the Hooded Figure, the Acolyte, the Sergeant's
ledgers, the Barkeep's memory, and the Busboy's observations should repeatedly
touch the critical path without requiring every scene for mechanical
progression.

### Optional World Arcs

The Realm of Cambion, Merzhin, Nimue, the Jester, the Red Dragon's layered
identities, summons, class quests, guild content, and unusual dungeon locations
may reinforce campaign themes while remaining optional or mechanically
parallel.

### Texture

Bounties, collection requests, merchant errands, parody titles, rumors, tips,
and eccentric encounters create breadth. They should not be mislabeled as main
story merely because they involve a boss or large reward.

## Player Choice Contract

The first narrative expansion should prefer a stable critical route with
remembered expression over large exclusive branches.

A story choice must define:

- what the player is deciding;
- whether the outcome is fixed or variable;
- who witnesses or later learns the answer;
- how the answer is stored;
- where it is echoed;
- what happens when old saves lack the field;
- whether the choice affects only prose or also mechanics;
- how every valid answer remains thematically legitimate.

The hero may fail combat or retry a trial without the narrative treating the
player's build as invalid. Failure copy should preserve dignity, explain the
route state, and return control cleanly.

## Active Narrative Debt

The following problems should be resolved through ordered story passes rather
than isolated line edits:

1. Tutorial instructions appear as ordinary NPC dialogue and break character.
2. Several active quests still invoke a generic "prime evil" or "primal evil"
   that belongs to an older story.
3. Too many unrelated boss contracts are classified as main quests without
   advancing one causal plot.
4. The Busboy lacks enough distinctive recurring characterization to earn the
   final reveal.
5. Joffrey is discussed more often than he is encountered.
6. The Acolyte has no meaningful trail through most of the game.
7. The repeated "Where's the Beef?" data can assign wedding dialogue to the
   Busboy even though the wedding belongs to the Waitress/Joffrey story.
8. Legacy Devil content remains in the shared event catalog for compatibility
   and must stay clearly separated from reachable Vesperion canon.
9. Core NPCs alternate between serious character writing and generic tutorial,
   reward, or quest-dispenser language.
10. The relic story jumps from first-relic mystery to one long collection
    objective, leaving the middle without chapter-level escalation.
11. Optional realms and bosses are mechanically rich but do not consistently
    reinforce, contrast, or deliberately step away from the central theme.
12. Copy age and quality vary substantially, including grammar errors, modern
    phrasing, and references that are not yet naturalized into the setting.

## First Narrative Vertical Slice

The first implementation-quality story packet should cover arrival through
Triangulus and the creation of The Holy Relics.

It should include:

- the existing intro, revised only if the opening storyboard identifies a
  concrete gap;
- a one-time arrival sequence at The Thirsty Dog;
- living introductions for the Barkeep, Waitress, Joffrey, Busboy, Sergeant,
  and an observed Hooded Figure;
- a clear reason the Sergeant trusts the hero with Uncertain Reports;
- patrol evidence in town and the first dungeon routes;
- an early human rescue or loss that establishes the cost of descent;
- a connected presentation of the Minotaur gate, Barghest lead, sealed chamber,
  and Triangulus;
- the first trace of the unnamed prior Acolyte;
- a distinct Triangulus discovery beat;
- a return-to-town reaction from several recurring characters;
- a subtle Busboy question about identity and responsibility;
- a Hooded Figure response that invites interpretation rather than commanding
  it;
- the report-back that establishes the six-relic possibility and creates The
  Holy Relics.

The slice should not change combat numbers, rewards, map topology, class rules,
or boss gates merely to support prose.

## Open Canon Decisions

These decisions are important enough to settle during story development rather
than infer during implementation:

- the proper names, relationships, and internal factions of the chthonic
  underworld's physical peoples and supernatural powers, which other recurring
  inter-realm groups deserve named histories, what motivates them, and what
  conditions stabilize or collapse smaller tears;
- the precise method Vesperion used to open the deeper threshold, the
  conditions that made it possible, and why he chose this moment;
- the names and prior relationships of the core Silvana cast;
- Joffrey's role, motive, and witnessed pre-tragedy scenes;
- the Acolyte's era, original purpose, and surviving trail;
- which existing bosses directly guard relic principles and which are
  obstacles or optional crises;
- how the Realm of Cambion and other side spaces relate historically to the
  main dungeon;
- whether the hero records an initial reason for coming to Silvana and how that
  answer returns later.

Routine travel, placement, connective dialogue, and minor staging should be
derived from settled canon without repeatedly reopening these major questions.

## Foundation Acceptance Criteria

The narrative foundation is ready for implementation when:

- the campaign has one causal act/chapter route;
- every relic has a town mirror, dungeon expression, confrontation, discovery,
  and aftermath;
- the Busboy, Hooded Figure, Acolyte, Waitress, Joffrey, Sergeant, and Barkeep
  have defined campaign functions;
- the Vesperion and Voluntas reveals are foreshadowed without being spoiled;
- humor has an explicit place and cannot override grief or revelation;
- active main quests can be assigned to a chapter or reclassified;
- fixed outcomes and variable expressions are distinguished;
- each chapter can produce an implementation packet with state, UI, content,
  test, and playtest requirements.
