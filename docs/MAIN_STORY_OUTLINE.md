# Main Story Outline

Status: **Campaign Board V1 — Narrative Planning Only**

This document converts the narrative foundation into a campaign-level route.
It is intentionally compact because **The Forsaken Tenet** remains a dungeon
crawler: chapters organize
meaningful changes in the existing descend-return loop rather than replacing
exploration with long linear scenes.

The proposed structure is a prologue, three acts, and ten chapters. Chapter
boundaries are narrative milestones, not necessarily separate maps or
mandatory title cards.

## Campaign Shape

| Section | Chapters | Primary movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue — Silvana's Plea | Prologue | Arrival and first voluntary step toward the dungeon. |
| Act I — The Opened Stair | 1–2 | Contradictory reports become proof of an older pattern. |
| Act II — Six Incomplete Truths | 3–7 | The remaining principles reshape the town, dungeon, and hero. |
| Act III — The Forsaken Tenet | 8–10 | The incomplete circle leads to defeat, Voluntas, return, and final choice. |

## Campaign-Level Invariants

- The player-created hero remains compatible with every legal race, sex, class,
  promotion, and Class Ring path.
- The six relics remain required for the final threshold.
- Vesperion remains the fallen Guardian of Voluntas and the Busboy in disguise.
- The Hooded Figure remains a Witness of Voluntas rather than the villain.
- Joffrey's death and the Waitress's grief remain real.
- The Acolyte remains a non-boss tragic mirror in the current route.
- The false final leads to The Liminal Gap.
- Guardian trials, Voluntas, Reflection, return, true final, and the ending
  retain their implemented order.
- Optional bosses and side realms must not become required retroactively
  without a separate gameplay and save contract.
- Humor may provide texture, but critical revelations remain sincere.

## Layered Descent Premise

The dungeon is one place with several histories.

1. **Silvana's upper works:** recently functional storage, drainage or water
   management, mining, refuge, burial, defensive, and controlled-access spaces.
2. **The buried stronghold or temple-city:** an older civilization that built
   around inherited sacred structures and preserved only partial knowledge of
   their purpose.
3. **The Guardian foundation:** the oldest complex, built by an ancient
   civilization rebuilding after devastating internal conflict and social
   near-collapse. Its survivors formalized seven mutually balancing safeguards
   from the failures that caused the crisis. They recognized seven people whose
   choices had already embodied those principles as the original Guardians and
   built spiritual custodial offices around those crisis exemplars. The
   Guardians protected the relics, history, and teachings but held no formal
   civil power. Each Guardian alone selected and trained a successor, making
   every transfer an act of personal judgment and trust. Across generations,
   private instruction preserved the core teachings but subtly narrowed each
   lineage's emphasis. Later factions exaggerated that cumulative drift into
   incompatible orthodoxies, although the final custodians rejected the
   extremists. The original Guardian of Voluntas followed the normal succession
   tradition by personally preparing Vesperion as the office's first successor.
   Later religions recast this history as a divine gift.
4. **Temporal contamination:** the rebuilt civilization eventually divided
   into movements that each treated one tenet as a complete answer. Their rival
   orthodoxies turned valid principles into weapons, producing a second civil
   war and social collapse. The other six Guardian traditions rejected every
   faction but withdrew into the Guardian complex, sealing the relics, records,
   and teachings while the civilization fell outside. When Vesperion performed
   the Hollowing, he knew the six custodians would choose to hold the seals and
   that doing so would kill them. He accepted their deaths as a necessary cost
   and proceeded. The six died protecting the complex after each voluntarily
   impressed a memory-echo into their relic. Vesperion treated both the
   recurrence and the retreat as proof that voluntary restraint could never
   work. The Hollowing damaged the boundaries between eras, tore the previously
   mortal Vesperion from natural life, made him ageless, and bound his existence
   to the resulting temporal wounds. Independent forces later exploited those
   wounds, introducing foreign creatures, technologies, environments, and side
   spaces into the descent without all of them serving Vesperion.

Each visible relic arc should preserve a distinct trace of that civil war: a
law, battlefield, memorial, propaganda fragment, corrupted rite, or ruined
community showing how a valid principle became destructive when treated as
complete. When possible, the evidence should distinguish the original balanced
teaching, the lineage's narrower inherited emphasis, and the faction's
absolutist distortion. It must condemn the severing of balance and choice rather
than imply that any principle is inherently evil.

The current crisis begins when Vesperion deliberately opens a deeper threshold
beyond the upper works Silvana knew how to use. He intends to attract explorers,
recover the six relics through their efforts, and provoke a final confrontation
over Voluntas. The town therefore has a real material relationship with the
dungeon while remaining ignorant of its full depth, original purpose, and
Vesperion's role in the opening.

## Prologue — The Lantern At The Edge

### Purpose

Establish Silvana as a place worth returning to, make the first descent a
choice rather than a menu transition, and introduce the campaign's emotional
and ideological cast before any twist matters.

### Mandatory Beats

1. The existing story cards establish the newly opened deeper threshold,
   missing expeditions, forgotten relics, and the hero's unspecified reason
   for coming.
2. The hero arrives at The Thirsty Dog during an ordinary evening strained by
   another incomplete patrol return.
3. The Barkeep manages the room; the Waitress and Joffrey are visibly together;
   the Busboy works at the edge of notice.
4. The Sergeant seeks reliable help because witness accounts disagree.
5. The Hooded Figure is seen but does not deliver an exposition speech.
6. The player receives a clear next action: speak with the Sergeant and examine
   Uncertain Reports.

### Optional Texture

- A patron makes a bad joke because nobody wants to discuss the empty place at
  the patrol table.
- The Drunkard recognizes a symbol described by a survivor, then retreats into
  humor when pressed.
- The Busboy remembers the hero's order or reason for being present with
  unsettling accuracy.
- A compact role-playing answer may record why the hero answered Silvana's
  plea, but only after its persistence and echo contract is approved.

### Information Boundary

The player knows the dungeon is old, lethal, and poorly understood. No one
knows there are exactly six relics. Vesperion and Voluntas are not named. The
Hooded Figure is suspicious only because of behavior and presentation.

### Handoff

The hero accepts Uncertain Reports and crosses the town threshold toward the
first descent.

## Act I — The Opened Stair

## Chapter 1 — Names In The Ledger

### Dramatic Purpose

Turn the dungeon from an abstract source of monsters into a place that consumes
specific people and produces unreliable evidence.

### Existing Gameplay Anchors

- Uncertain Reports;
- Bring Him Home;
- Rookie Mistake;
- early dungeon exploration;
- the Minotaur route and first-floor access;
- first meetings with town services.

These anchors do not all need to become mandatory. Timmy and the Rookie can
serve as contrasting optional outcomes: one person brought home alive, another
brought home for burial.

### Story Progression

1. The Sergeant shows the hero patrol entries describing sealed rooms, old
   symbols, missing hours, and incompatible routes.
2. Joffrey appears in the barracks or tavern as an active defender preparing
   for his own work below.
3. The hero follows marks left by multiple patrols and discovers that
   contradictions repeat in a pattern.
4. Early rescue and recovery content demonstrates that returning a person or
   their name matters to Silvana.
5. The Minotaur is framed as a brutal gate in the same crisis, not an unrelated
   contract placed between the player and the next level.
6. A small trace from an earlier explorer appears without the title Acolyte:
   disciplined notes, an unusual symbol, or a line about needing proof of who
   they were.

### Town Change

The barracks ledger adds or resolves named entries. The Barkeep and Waitress
recognize that the hero returned with more than loot. Joffrey becomes more
interested in the hero's report rather than existing only in secondhand
dialogue.

### Information Gained

The reports are not random mistakes. Something in the dungeon affects
perception, memory, or the way routes are described.

### Information Withheld

The player does not yet know whether the dungeon is alive, cursed, deliberately
misdirecting explorers, or preserving several overlapping truths.

### Handoff

One persistent report points toward a shape-changing creature near a sealed
chamber: the Barghest lead in Cry Havoc!.

## Chapter 2 — The Shape Of Proof

### Principle

**Triangulus — Self**

### Dramatic Purpose

Confirm the relic mystery while asking whether identity is a fixed fact or an
answer repeatedly chosen.

### Existing Gameplay Anchors

- Cry Havoc!;
- Barghest and its changing forms;
- the first sealed relic chamber;
- Triangulus discovery;
- Uncertain Reports report-back;
- creation of The Holy Relics.

### Story Progression

1. Witnesses give incompatible descriptions of the same Barghest because its
   form changes with expectation and fear.
2. The hero reaches evidence left by the prior explorer. The fragment treats a
   name, role, or class as proof of identity, foreshadowing a later crisis.
3. The Barghest encounter turns the chapter's uncertainty into a concrete
   confrontation.
4. Triangulus responds to mind, body, and remembered choice.
5. Relic discovery clarifies that the old patrol accounts described one
   repeating structure rather than unrelated rooms.
6. Returning the evidence lets the Sergeant assemble a possible six-part
   pattern and create The Holy Relics.

### Recurring Character Responses

- **Sergeant:** accepts the evidence but resists claiming certainty beyond it.
- **Joffrey:** sees proof that ordinary people must act rather than wait.
- **Waitress:** worries that necessity is becoming an excuse for people she
  loves to enter the dungeon.
- **Busboy:** asks whether the hero defeated the creature by discovering what it
  was or by deciding what it meant.
- **Hooded Figure:** suggests that a chosen name can become a vow, but never
  tells the hero which identity to choose.
- **Barkeep:** notices the room reacting to relic-light before anyone speaks.

### Fixed Outcome

Triangulus is recovered and the broader relic route opens.

### Variable Expression

The player may later remember whether they emphasized name, endurance, memory,
duty, curiosity, or another approved identity answer. No answer invalidates a
class or changes the chapter's required outcome.

### Handoff

Silvana knows there may be six relics. It does not know whether they are
weapons, keys, warnings, or pieces of a damaged doctrine.

## Act II — Six Incomplete Truths

Act II should occupy most of the game. Each chapter uses existing floor gates,
bosses, locations, and quests, but the exact mechanical mapping must be audited
before a chapter packet changes content. The outline defines narrative
function; it does not move a boss or relic by assertion.

## Chapter 3 — The Measure Of A Cage

### Principle

**Quadrata — Order**

### Human Conflict

The first relic makes Silvana respond with new rules: stricter patrol control,
restricted routes, confiscated keys, rationed supplies, or pressure to place all
dungeon knowledge under one authority. The measures are understandable and may
save lives, but they also reveal how fear turns protection into command.

The Sergeant should not become a tyrant. His arc is the burden of deciding how
much order a frightened town needs and recognizing when procedure begins
treating people as replaceable pieces.

### Dungeon Expression

Measured paths, constructs, keys, route sequencing, or a boss that enforces one
acceptable pattern can embody order without consent. Existing Iron Golem,
warehouse, soldier, key, and engineered-dungeon material are candidates, but
the final chapter packet must respect current progression order.

### Acolyte Trace

The prior explorer's notes become cleaner and more rigid. They begin recording
rules that promise safety if followed perfectly.

### Choice Space

The hero may obey a useful rule, challenge a harmful one, or rewrite a process
to preserve its purpose. Later dialogue remembers the reasoning, not a
good/evil score.

### Relic Aftermath

Quadrata reveals that structure can shelter people only while those people
retain the ability to question and reshape it.

## Chapter 4 — What The Living Choose

### Principle

**Hexagonum — Nature**

### Human Conflict

Silvana debates whether the dungeon's living systems are resources, threats,
victims, or evidence. Alchemists need reagents. The town needs food and
medicine. Fungal growth, springs, beasts, curses, and hidden routes resist one
simple moral category.

Seraphine Voss and Mara Vale can provide informed but different interpretations
without either becoming the author's mouthpiece.

### Dungeon Expression

The Underground Spring, fungal passages, communion locations, beasts, and
nature-linked enemies should show adaptation rather than a generic green-biome
chapter. The player sees organisms surviving inside several unnatural
historical layers.

### Acolyte Trace

The prior explorer writes that hunger, fear, or strength proves what a creature
is. A later annotation questions that conclusion, showing the beginning of
internal fracture.

### Choice Space

Remain, defend, adapt, harvest carefully, or refuse an unnecessary act of
destruction. Mechanical harvesting remains separately gated; this chapter can
first express the decision through story and existing interactions.

### Relic Aftermath

Hexagonum establishes that instinct begins the path but cannot dictate its
meaning.

## Chapter 5 — Love's Open Hand

### Principle

**Luna — Love**

### Dramatic Purpose

Make the Waitress/Joffrey tragedy the emotional center of the campaign rather
than a late quest reveal used primarily to support Vesperion's final speech.

### Required Pre-Tragedy Work

Before Joffrey descends on his fatal route, the player must:

- meet him;
- understand why he believes he must act;
- see his relationship with the Waitress during an ordinary moment;
- understand that she respects his agency while fearing its cost;
- observe the Busboy offer sympathy or advice that can later be read as
  pressure;
- receive enough information that Joffrey's later letter refers to known
  tensions rather than inventing them after his death.

### Fixed Tragic Outcome

Joffrey dies in the current campaign route. Vesperion may influence his
information, fear, timing, or sense of obligation, but Joffrey is not reduced
to a puppet. His love and fatal choice remain his.

### Variable Expression

The hero may vary:

- how urgently they search;
- which evidence they understand first;
- how the truth is delivered;
- whether they answer the Waitress with comfort, honesty, anger, or silence;
- which interpretation the town carries afterward.

The first pass need not make survival variable.

### Dungeon Expression

Luna's chamber should echo holding, releasing, protection, obligation, and the
difference between sacrifice offered freely and sacrifice demanded by love.

### Acolyte Trace

The prior explorer writes about someone they tried to protect by making every
decision for them. The fragment shows how love became justification for
control.

### Busboy Function

The Busboy's apparently compassionate question becomes sharper: would it have
been kinder if Joffrey had never been allowed to choose the road that killed
him?

### Relic Aftermath

Luna answers that love without freedom becomes possession, duty, or fear. It
does not make the grief disappear.

## Chapter 6 — The Star That Does Not Command

### Principle

**Polaris — Guidance**

### Human Conflict

By this point the hero has several sources of advice: the Sergeant's reports,
the Hooded Figure's riddles, the Busboy's sympathy, guild routes, maps,
scientists, Nimue, and other dungeon survivors. The problem is no longer a lack
of guidance but deciding which guidance preserves judgment.

### Dungeon Expression

False paths, the Jester's games, Realm of Cambion portals, altered maps, hidden
routes, and anti-magic clues are natural supporting material. Not all must
become mandatory. Their shared purpose is to test whether the hero follows,
questions, remembers, or chooses a route with incomplete evidence.

### Hooded Figure Development

The Hooded Figure should become more useful without becoming less unsettling.
Their credibility grows because they leave room for refusal and admit limits,
not because the story declares them trustworthy.

### Acolyte Trace

The prior explorer stops evaluating instructions and begins referring to one
voice as the only safe guide.

### Busboy Function

The Busboy offers clearer, easier conclusions. His advice should often be
practical enough to tempt the player while quietly narrowing the perceived
range of choices.

### Relic Aftermath

Polaris establishes that guidance deserves trust only when it leaves the
traveler capable of asking why.

## Chapter 7 — The Choice To Continue

### Principle

**Infinitas — Endurance**

### Human Conflict

Silvana is exhausted. Its ledgers are full, supplies are strained, and
continued descent no longer feels automatically heroic. The player should see
people who continue from courage, guilt, habit, fear, lack of alternatives, and
genuine renewed purpose.

The Drunkard's attempt to stop drinking should remain imperfect but sincere.
It provides a human-scale expression of endurance: continuing life can mean
ending a destructive repetition.

### Dungeon Expression

The final relic route, Cerberus, loops, attrition, repeated thresholds, and
long-standing defenders can embody endurance. The chapter should ask when
persistence is resolve and when it is merely an inability to stop.

### Acolyte Trace

The last pre-Liminal fragment reveals that the prior explorer no longer wanted
to decide whether to continue. They asked another will to decide for them.

### Choice Space

Continue, rest, ask for help, acknowledge fear, or reject a false version of
heroism. The route still proceeds, but the answer can return during Reflection
and Vesperion's argument.

### Relic Aftermath

Infinitas completes the visible circle while revealing that endurance without
the freedom to stop is only captivity stretched across time.

## Act III — The Forsaken Tenet

## Chapter 8 — The Closing Circle

### Dramatic Purpose

Transform collection completion into a consequential final approach and expose
the fact that the six-part pattern is deliberately incomplete.

### Story Progression

1. The sixth relic returns to Silvana.
2. The Sergeant's ledgers align for the first time and reveal a repeated empty
   space rather than a complete answer.
3. Town reactions mix hope, dread, pride, fatigue, and unresolved grief.
4. The Busboy becomes difficult to find or leaves one final apparently ordinary
   observation.
5. The Barkeep realizes no one can clearly remember when the Busboy began
   working at The Thirsty Dog. This is a clue, not yet a full reveal.
6. The Hooded Figure warns that the final threshold recognizes the relics but
   refuses to explain the missing place.
7. The hero enters the final chamber and confronts Vesperion.
8. The six relics answer incompletely.
9. The false-final fight reaches its scripted turn.
10. Vesperion defeats the hero and casts them into The Liminal Gap.

### Fixed Outcome

The player cannot win the first confrontation. The scene must feel like earned
revelation and catastrophic incompleteness rather than an arbitrary unwinnable
fight.

### Information Gained

Vesperion is the will behind the final threshold and argues that choice creates
suffering. The relics preserve something, but not enough.

### Information Withheld

The exact missing principle, Vesperion's former Guardian identity, the Hooded
Figure's full role, the Acolyte's final state, and the Busboy truth remain for
the Liminal and true-final sequence.

## Chapter 9 — The Empty Seventh Seat

### Dramatic Purpose

Let the player reinterpret the entire campaign through principles they already
encountered, affirm the hero's chosen identity, and learn why the six truths
were incomplete.

### Existing Route Anchors

- Liminal Gap arrival and hub;
- wounded Hooded Figure guide;
- six Guardian memory-echo trials and deeper vignettes;
- clue review;
- Empty Seventh Seat;
- Voluntas reveal;
- Acolyte mirror;
- Class Ring and class-identity affirmation;
- Reflection/Psychopomp;
- unnamed witness farewell;
- return from The Liminal Gap.

### Story Progression

1. The wounded Hooded Figure admits guidance, misdirection, and withheld truth.
2. Each Guardian trial is an intentional memory-echo left by a custodian who
   died holding the seals. It recalls a conflict already lived in Silvana and
   the dungeon rather than presenting an isolated philosophy lecture.
3. The player's earlier answers and build receive recognition where available.
4. Six clues expose the deliberately empty seventh place.
5. The Hooded Figure reveals Vesperion as the original Voluntas Guardian's
   personally prepared first successor, the second collapse and Guardian
   withdrawal that broke his faith in voluntary restraint, his knowing
   acceptance of the six custodians' deaths, and how the Hollowing made him
   ageless by binding him to its temporal wounds.
6. Voluntas is understood as choosing, not collected as an object.
7. The Acolyte confirms the cost of surrendering choice.
8. Reflection tests whether the hero can affirm the path actually taken rather
   than an idealized alternate self.
9. Victory opens the return.

### Acolyte Payoff

Fragments from earlier chapters should make the Acolyte recognizable before
the title is spoken. Their Liminal scene completes a trajectory the player has
been reading throughout the game.

The Acolyte does not need immediate redemption, combat, or a heroic sacrifice.
Their existence is the warning: peace purchased by surrendering the self
eventually leaves no one present to experience peace.

### Fixed Outcome

Voluntas is revealed, Reflection is defeated, and the true-final return opens.

### Variable Expression

Guardian answers, class identity, Class Ring state, prior role-playing answers,
and selected town responses may change prose and echoes without changing the
legitimacy of the route.

## Chapter 10 — The Evening Star

### Dramatic Purpose

Resolve the philosophical conflict through everything the hero chose and
endured, then return attention to the ordinary people who must live after the
victory.

### Story Progression

1. The hero returns to the final threshold by choice rather than accident.
2. Vesperion recognizes the restored pattern and the self the hero affirmed.
3. The Busboy disguise is fully acknowledged.
4. Vesperion defends accepting the six custodians' deaths, then reframes the
   second collapse, Joffrey, the Waitress, the Acolyte, and the town's losses as
   proof that freedom is cruelty.
5. The hero's answer is expressed through accumulated actions and selected
   echoes rather than one canon speech assigned to every build.
6. Guardian counters and Choose Fate turn prior story trials into readable
   final-battle consequences.
7. Vesperion's defeat preserves Voluntas without awarding it as a possession.
8. The ending moves cleanly from final victory to the Forsaken Tenet revelation
   and The Thirsty Dog epilogue.
9. The town remains scarred. Joffrey remains dead. The Busboy's absence has a
   physical and social shape.
10. Ordinary life resumes with more freedom, not perfect safety.

### Ending Image

The strongest ending image should be small rather than cosmic: a choice made in
The Thirsty Dog after the Evening Star is gone. Someone lights the window
candle, leaves a chair empty, opens the door, or chooses to descend for their
own reason. The world continues because choice continues.

## Main Quest Reclassification Rule

A quest belongs to the main story only when completing it causes at least one
of the following:

- advances access to a relic or the final route;
- changes the town's understanding of the dungeon;
- changes a central character relationship;
- reveals or materially complicates the Guardian pattern;
- advances the Vesperion, Hooded Figure, Acolyte, Joffrey, or Voluntas arc;
- creates the causal handoff to the next chapter.

A difficult boss, large reward, floor gate, or memorable joke is not sufficient
by itself. Such content can remain mechanically important while being labeled
as a supporting or side quest.

## Per-Chapter Production Packet

Each chapter should receive one reconciled packet before implementation:

1. **Chapter contract:** purpose, prerequisites, entry state, exit state, and
   non-goals.
2. **Scene board:** order, location, viewpoint, participants, player control,
   mandatory revelations, optional texture, information withheld, failure
   handling, and handoff.
3. **Cast authority:** what each participant knows, wants, fears, lies about,
   and may reveal.
4. **Dialogue script:** all mandatory copy plus conditional variants.
5. **Quest mapping:** accepted, updated, completed, reclassified, or retired
   quest entries.
6. **World mapping:** existing rooms, tiles, bosses, and town surfaces used by
   the chapter.
7. **State contract:** flags, defaults, repeat behavior, old-save behavior, and
   reset policy.
8. **Consequence matrix:** fixed outcomes, remembered expressions, optional
   discoveries, and mechanical effects if separately approved.
9. **Presentation contract:** story cards, dialogue, logs, environmental text,
   sound, and input behavior.
10. **Validation:** content tests, route tests, save round trips, regression
    targets, and manual playtest prompts.
11. **Canon reconciliation:** narrative foundation, timeline, reveal schedule,
    docs index, and roadmap status updated together.

Shared events should have one owning chapter. Other chapters may echo them but
must not repeat the same revelation as if it were new.

## Implementation Order

### Narrative Slice 1 — Arrival Through Triangulus

Build the Prologue, Chapter 1, and Chapter 2 as one end-to-end vertical slice.
This is the first test of whether story integration improves engagement during
actual play.

### Narrative Slice 2 — Quadrata And Hexagonum

Establish the repeatable principle-arc structure, town-state reactions, and
Acolyte trail before authoring the most emotionally consequential material.

### Narrative Slice 3 — Luna

Complete Joffrey and the Waitress's pre-tragedy authority, dialogue, fixed
outcome, variable expressions, and aftermath as a dedicated packet.

### Narrative Slice 4 — Polaris And Infinitas

Pay off the Hooded Figure/Busboy contrast, integrate optional illusion and
guidance content, and carry the Acolyte trail to the edge of surrender.

### Narrative Slice 5 — Closing Circle And Endgame Reconciliation

Connect six-relic completion, false final, Liminal route, true final, and
epilogue as one continuous sequence. Preserve existing mechanics unless a
separate promoted spec authorizes a change.

### Narrative Slice 6 — Supporting And Texture Pass

Reclassify remaining quests, naturalize references, separate tutorials from NPC
voices, repair grammar and continuity, and deepen optional realms without
diluting the critical route.

## Campaign Board Exit Criteria

The campaign outline is ready to become production storyboards when:

- the current dungeon progression order is mapped to all ten chapters;
- every existing main quest is assigned, reclassified, or identified as debt;
- the chronology and mechanics of the temporal incursions, and the precise
  method Vesperion used to open the deeper threshold, are settled;
- the central cast naming and authority pass is complete;
- Joffrey's living scenes and fatal route are causally complete;
- the Acolyte's fragment sequence is defined;
- each relic's boss, chamber, town mirror, and aftermath are identified;
- optional realms have explicit thematic and progression roles;
- the Prologue-through-Triangulus packet can be written without inventing new
  canon during implementation.
